12/**
13 * @brief Utility class replacing every literal in a text by the number encoding it.
14 *
15 * The engine represents a string by its index in the string registry and a literal array or value with
16 * fields by the index of the constant object it states in the object registry, so that every value is a
17 * number. Text stating such a value must therefore be
18 * scanned before it is read as a value or parsed as an expression, and this class is the one place in
19 * which that is done, so that what a lexeme is is decided once.
20 *
21 * The text is read once, from left to right, and each thing met is dealt with where it is met.
22 *
23 * A quoted span is read to its closing quote and emitted as its index in the string registry. A quote
24 * within it does not occur, quotes not being escaped, and a comma or a bracket within it is text rather
25 * than structure.
26 *
27 * A bracket either opens an array literal or belongs to the expression it stands in, and which of the two
28 * is decided by what has been emitted last. After a name, a closing bracket or a closing parenthesis it
29 * indexes, and after a membership operator it states a set the expression parser reads itself, so in both
30 * cases the bracketed text is copied and its contents are scanned where they stand. Everywhere else the
31 * bracket opens a literal. A brace opens a literal with fields, `{ name := value, ... }`, where a literal may
32 * stand and a name followed by `:=` follows it; any other brace is a set or the body of an aggregation and is
33 * copied. The members of an array and the values of the fields are read one by one, each by the same rules,
34 * so that each is a quoted string, a truth value, a number, or a literal of its own, to any depth. A numeric
35 * value is recorded as a decimal, so that a literal may hold whole and fractional numbers alike and both are
36 * written out as they were read. The members of an array are uniform: they agree in type and, being arrays
37 * or values with fields themselves, in the sizes of their arrays and in their fields. The literal is
38 * registered in the object registry as a constant object, stored flat, and emitted as its index there.
39 *
40 * Everything else, which is to say names, numbers, operators, whitespace, and the delimiters of a line,
41 * is copied unchanged, a membership operator included. Of the grammar the expression parser applies, the
42 * scan knows what decides a bracket and nothing besides, so that the text it emits is read as the text
43 * it was given.
44 *
45 * A text that cannot be read is refused, naming it: a quote that is not closed, a literal that is not
46 * closed, an array without members, a member without a value, a member that is neither a truth value nor a
47 * number nor a literal, members that are not uniform, and a field without a name, without `:=` or stated
48 * twice.
49 */
